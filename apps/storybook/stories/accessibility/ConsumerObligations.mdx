  <SectionHead
    title="Why partial applicability is honest"
    meta="component vs. application boundary"
  />
  <p>
    A component library cannot single-handedly ship AAA conformance. WCAG defines accessibility
    against a <em>web page</em>, not a button or a dialog in isolation. For 7 of the 28 AAA success
    criteria, HELiX&apos;s contribution is real but partial: we expose the API, you populate it.
    Calling these &quot;done&quot; without naming the consumer&apos;s share would be marketing — so
    we publish both ledgers. If a consumer ships a HELiX-based product without fulfilling these
    contracts, the page is not AAA, even if every component is.
  </p>
  <p>
    See{' '}
    <a href="/?path=/docs/accessibility-success-criteria--docs">Accessibility / Success Criteria</a>{' '}
    for the fully-applicable AAA SCs HELiX owns end-to-end, and the &quot;What we do not claim&quot;
    section for the 14 not-applicable AAA SCs (sign language, reading level, pronunciation, audio
    description) that fall outside any component-library scope.
  </p>
</section>

<section>
  <SectionHead title="1.3.6 Identify Purpose" meta="AAA · partial · consumer fulfills" />

<DocsCard title="What WCAG 2.1 1.3.6 requires" tag="user-agent identifies purpose">
  <p>
    In content implemented using markup languages, the purpose of UI components, icons, and regions
    can be programmatically determined. This lets assistive tech and personalization tools surface
    the right meaning (e.g. recolor &quot;cancel&quot; vs &quot;submit&quot;, replace icons with
    words, autofill a name field correctly).
  </p>
</DocsCard>

<DocsCard title="What HELiX provides" tag="component contract">
  <p>
    <code>hx-text-input</code>, <code>hx-textarea</code>, <code>hx-select</code>, and
    <code>hx-combobox</code> all forward the native <code>autocomplete</code> attribute through
    ElementInternals; consumers populate it from the WCAG-defined token list (Section 7 of WCAG
    2.1). Buttons and links accept <code>aria-label</code> + <code>aria-roledescription</code> for
    semantic intent beyond the visible text. Icon components honor <code>title</code> and
    <code>aria-labelledby</code>.
  </p>
</DocsCard>

  <DocsCard title="What you fulfill" tag="consumer code">
    <p>
      Populate <code>autocomplete</code> on every form control whose purpose matches Section 7&apos;s
      input-purpose tokens. A signup form should look like:
    </p>
    <CodeBlock
      language="html"
      code={`<hx-text-input name="given-name" autocomplete="given-name" label="First name"></hx-text-input>
<hx-text-input name="family-name" autocomplete="family-name" label="Last name"></hx-text-input>
<hx-text-input name="email" type="email" autocomplete="email" label="Email"></hx-text-input>
<hx-text-input name="tel" type="tel" autocomplete="tel-national" label="Phone"></hx-text-input>`}
    />
  </DocsCard>
</section>

<section>
  <SectionHead title="1.4.8 Visual Presentation" meta="AAA · partial · consumer fulfills" />

<DocsCard title="What WCAG 2.1 1.4.8 requires" tag="user-controllable presentation">
  <p>For blocks of text, the user can:</p>
  <ul>
    <li>Select foreground and background colors</li>
    <li>Limit width to 80 characters or less</li>
    <li>Avoid full justification (no fully-justified text)</li>
    <li>Set line-spacing to at least 1.5 within paragraphs</li>
    <li>Set paragraph spacing to at least 1.5x line-spacing</li>
    <li>Resize text to 200% without horizontal scroll</li>
  </ul>
</DocsCard>

<DocsCard title="What HELiX provides" tag="component contract">
  <p>
    <code>hx-prose</code> defaults to <code>line-height: 1.6</code> on body text and
    <code>paragraph spacing: 1.5em</code> (clears the 1.5x line-spacing rule). Width is bounded by
    <code>max-inline-size: var(--hx-prose-measure, 70ch)</code> — under the 80-char ceiling. Text
    alignment is <code>start</code> by default; we never emit <code>text-align: justify</code>.
    Color tokens (<code>--hx-color-text-primary</code>, <code>--hx-color-surface-default</code>) are
    user-overridable at the semantic tier — set them once on the consumer&apos;s root and every
    component recolors via cascade.
  </p>
</DocsCard>

  <DocsCard title="What you fulfill" tag="consumer code">
    <p>
      Do not override <code>hx-prose</code> defaults to make text denser. If you do override
      typography globally, preserve the line-height and paragraph-spacing minimums. Do not apply
      <code>text-align: justify</code> to body content.
    </p>
    <CodeBlock
      language="css"
      code={[
        '/* OK: narrows measure but stays under the 80ch ceiling */',
        'hx-prose { --hx-prose-measure: 60ch; }',
        '',
        '/* OK: global type-scale override that preserves AAA minimums */',
        ':root {',
        '  --hx-text-line-height: 1.6;       /* >= 1.5 */',
        '  --hx-text-paragraph-spacing: 1em; /* >= 0.5 * line-height */',
        '}',
        '',
        '/* NOT OK: kills the line-height floor */',
        'hx-prose { line-height: 1.2 !important; }',
        '',
        '/* NOT OK: full justification fails 1.4.8 */',
        'hx-prose { text-align: justify; }',
      ].join('\n')}
    />

  </DocsCard>
</section>

<section>
  <SectionHead title="2.2.4 Interruptions" meta="AAA · partial · consumer fulfills" />

<DocsCard title="What WCAG 2.1 2.2.4 requires" tag="user can suppress">
  <p>
    Interruptions (alerts, banners, toasts, system notifications) can be postponed or suppressed by
    the user, except interruptions involving an emergency.
  </p>
</DocsCard>

<DocsCard title="What HELiX provides" tag="component contract">
  <p>
    <code>hx-toast</code>, <code>hx-banner</code>, and <code>hx-alert</code> all fire dismissible
    events and accept a <code>persistent</code> attribute that disables auto-dismiss.
    <code>hx-toast</code> exposes a <code>duration</code> property (any positive number, or
    <code>Infinity</code> for &quot;until dismissed&quot;). The library reads
    <code>data-hx-no-interruptions</code> on the document root: when set, non-emergency toasts stay
    queued instead of rendering until cleared. Emergency-priority toasts (severity &quot;error&quot;
    with <code>persistent</code>) bypass the suppression.
  </p>
</DocsCard>

  <DocsCard title="What you fulfill" tag="consumer code">
    <p>
      Wire a user-controllable preference (settings page, OS-level preference) to
      <code>data-hx-no-interruptions</code>. Do not auto-dismiss <em>error</em> alerts about
      patient-care or safety conditions without an explicit user action.
    </p>
    <CodeBlock
      language="html"
      code={`<!-- Consumer settings UI toggles this attribute -->
<html data-hx-no-interruptions>
  <body>
    <!-- routine toast queues silently until cleared -->
    <hx-toast variant="info" duration="3000">Settings saved</hx-toast>

    <!-- emergency toast still surfaces -->
    <hx-toast variant="error" persistent>
      Glucose reading 38 mg/dL — confirm patient response
    </hx-toast>

  </body>
</html>`}
    />
  </DocsCard>
</section>

<section>
  <SectionHead title="2.2.6 Timeouts" meta="AAA · partial · consumer fulfills" />

<DocsCard title="What WCAG 2.1 2.2.6 requires" tag="user warned of data loss">
  <p>
    Users are warned of the duration of any user inactivity that could cause data loss, unless the
    data is preserved for more than 20 hours when the user does not take any actions.
  </p>
</DocsCard>

<DocsCard title="What HELiX provides" tag="component contract">
  <p>
    <code>hx-dialog</code> accepts a <code>persistent</code> attribute that disables click-outside
    and Escape dismissal — useful for unsaved-form-state warnings. <code>hx-toast</code> exposes
    <code>duration</code> + <code>persistent</code> for the inverse case (notifications that should
    not auto-disappear). <code>prefers-reduced-motion: reduce</code> automatically pauses
    auto-dismiss timers across <code>hx-toast</code>, <code>hx-banner</code>,
    <code>hx-carousel</code>, and any timed transition. The library does not impose session timeouts
    — those are the consumer application&apos;s concern.
  </p>
</DocsCard>

  <DocsCard title="What you fulfill" tag="consumer code">
    <p>
      Surface a warning before any session-timeout-driven data loss. Use <code>hx-dialog</code>
      with <code>persistent</code> + a countdown to give the user a chance to extend, save, or
      discard.
    </p>
    <CodeBlock
      language="html"
      code={`<hx-dialog persistent open id="session-timeout">
  <h2 slot="header">Your session is about to expire</h2>
  <p>
    You will be signed out in 2 minutes. Unsaved changes to this patient record
    will be lost.
  </p>
  <hx-button slot="footer" variant="primary" onclick="extendSession()">
    Stay signed in
  </hx-button>
  <hx-button slot="footer" variant="ghost" onclick="saveDraftAndSignOut()">
    Save draft and sign out
  </hx-button>
</hx-dialog>`}
    />
  </DocsCard>
</section>

<section>
  <SectionHead title="2.4.10 Section Headings" meta="AAA · partial · consumer fulfills" />

<DocsCard title="What WCAG 2.1 2.4.10 requires" tag="content structure">
  <p>
    Section headings are used to organize the content. Headings should follow a logical hierarchy
    (no skipped levels) and describe the section that follows.
  </p>
</DocsCard>

<DocsCard title="What HELiX provides" tag="component contract">
  <p>
    <code>hx-prose</code> renders consumer-supplied <code>&lt;h1&gt;</code>—<code>&lt;h6&gt;</code>
    with the correct visual hierarchy and clear margin rhythm, but it does <em>not</em>
    auto-determine heading levels — that is the consumer&apos;s page-structure decision.
    <code>hx-text</code> exposes a <code>level</code> attribute for visual heading sizes that are{' '}
    <em>not</em> document-outline headings (e.g. a card title that visually looks like an h3 but
    isn&apos;t part of the page outline). Component slots like <code>hx-card[slot=header]</code>,
    <code>hx-dialog[slot=header]</code>, and <code>hx-drawer[slot=header]</code> accept any heading
    element so the consumer chooses the level.
  </p>
</DocsCard>

  <DocsCard title="What you fulfill" tag="consumer code">
    <p>
      Author your page&apos;s heading hierarchy explicitly. Do not skip levels. Use
      <code>hx-text level=&quot;h3&quot;</code> for visual-only heading sizes that are not part of
      the outline.
    </p>
    <CodeBlock
      language="html"
      code={`<main>
  <h1>Patient overview</h1>

  <section>
    <h2>Vitals</h2>
    <hx-card>
      <!-- Card heading IS part of the outline → real h3 -->
      <h3 slot="header">Heart rate</h3>
      <hx-stat value="72 bpm" />
    </hx-card>

    <hx-card>
      <!-- Decorative subheading not in outline → hx-text level="h4" -->
      <hx-text slot="header" level="h4">Last reading</hx-text>
      <hx-format-date value="2026-05-07T08:31:00Z" />
    </hx-card>

  </section>

  <section>
    <h2>Active medications</h2>
    <!-- ... -->
  </section>
</main>`}
    />
  </DocsCard>
</section>

<section>
  <SectionHead title="3.1.4 Abbreviations" meta="AAA · partial · consumer fulfills" />

<DocsCard title="What WCAG 2.1 3.1.4 requires" tag="expansion available">
  <p>
    A mechanism for identifying the expanded form or meaning of abbreviations is available. In
    practice this is the <code>&lt;abbr title=&quot;…&quot;&gt;</code> element on first use, or a
    glossary linked from the page.
  </p>
</DocsCard>

<DocsCard title="What HELiX provides" tag="component contract">
  <p>
    <code>hx-prose</code> styles native <code>&lt;abbr&gt;</code> with a subtle dotted-underline
    affordance and surfaces the <code>title</code> via the browser&apos;s native tooltip plus
    <code>aria-label</code> when consumers attach one. <code>hx-tooltip</code> provides a richer
    surface for explanations longer than a single phrase. The library does not own content — we do
    not auto-detect abbreviations.
  </p>
</DocsCard>

  <DocsCard title="What you fulfill" tag="consumer code">
    <p>
      Wrap every abbreviation in <code>&lt;abbr&gt;</code> on first occurrence per page or per
      section. For domain-heavy content (medical, legal), maintain a glossary route the
      abbreviation links to.
    </p>
    <CodeBlock
      language="html"
      code={`<hx-prose>
  <p>
    Patient presents with elevated <abbr title="Blood Pressure">BP</abbr>
    (148/96) and tachycardic <abbr title="Heart Rate">HR</abbr> at 112 bpm.
    Order <abbr title="Electrocardiogram">ECG</abbr> stat.
  </p>
</hx-prose>

<!-- For a richer explanation, hx-tooltip wraps the abbr -->

<hx-tooltip>
  <abbr slot="anchor" title="Glasgow Coma Scale">GCS</abbr>
  <p>
    A 15-point scale used to assess level of consciousness. 15 = fully alert,
    3 = deep coma. Scored on eye, verbal, and motor responses.
  </p>
</hx-tooltip>`}
    />
  </DocsCard>
</section>

<section>
  <SectionHead title="3.3.5 Help" meta="AAA · partial · consumer fulfills" />

<DocsCard title="What WCAG 2.1 3.3.5 requires" tag="context-sensitive help">
  <p>
    Context-sensitive help is available for any component that requires user input. For forms in
    regulated contexts (legal, financial, medical), help should explain what the input is for and
    the format expected.
  </p>
</DocsCard>

<DocsCard title="What HELiX provides" tag="component contract">
  <p>
    Every form component (<code>hx-text-input</code>, <code>hx-textarea</code>,
    <code>hx-select</code>, <code>hx-combobox</code>, <code>hx-checkbox</code>,
    <code>hx-radio-group</code>, <code>hx-date-picker</code>, etc.) accepts a<code>helpText</code>{' '}
    attribute and a <code>help-text</code> slot for richer markup.
    <code>hx-help-text</code> is the canonical primitive — it wires
    <code>aria-describedby</code> through ElementInternals so the help text is announced after the
    field label by every screen reader. <code>hx-tooltip</code> + <code>hx-popover</code>
    surface longer explanations on demand. Field-level <code>hx-field-label</code> renders the
    required-marker and links the label-input pair via Shadow DOM-internal <code>for/id</code>.
  </p>
</DocsCard>

  <DocsCard title="What you fulfill" tag="consumer code">
    <p>
      Populate <code>help-text</code> with substantive content — what the field is for, the format
      expected, and where to find more information. Do not leave help text empty on
      compliance-critical fields.
    </p>
    <CodeBlock
      language="html"
      code={`<!-- OK — substantive help that meets 3.3.5 -->
<hx-text-input
  name="ssn"
  label="Social Security Number"
  autocomplete="off"
  required
>
  <span slot="help-text">
    Format: 9 digits, no dashes (e.g. 123456789). Used only to verify
    insurance eligibility — not stored on this device.
  </span>
</hx-text-input>

<!-- For long-form help, hx-popover keeps the field uncluttered -->

<hx-text-input name="advance-directive" label="Advance directive on file?">
  <hx-popover slot="help-text">
    <button slot="anchor" type="button" aria-label="What is this?">?</button>
    <p>
      An advance directive is a legal document specifying your healthcare
      wishes if you cannot communicate them yourself. See the
      <a href="/help/advance-directives">advance-directive help page</a>
      for details.
    </p>
  </hx-popover>
</hx-text-input>`}
    />
  </DocsCard>
</section>
